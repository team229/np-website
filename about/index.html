How this started

My introduction to tarot wasn't dramatic. It was curiosity that turned into study, and study that turned into something I couldn't put down. Over time it became clear that the useful part wasn't prediction — it was the way a reading could take something tangled and make it speakable.

Seven years and more than 1,600 clients later, that's still the part I care about. People arrive with a situation they've been carrying for months. Often they already know what they need to do. What they don't have is language for it, or permission to trust it.

What I actually do

I read tarot, numerology and Vedic charts. Over the years I've also built a practice around intention-based rituals — candle work, and the oils and crystals that support it. Those exist because clients kept asking what they could do between sessions, and I wanted the answer to be something disciplined rather than a shopping list.

What I won't do

  • Promise outcomes. No reading can guarantee what another person will do.
  • Sell fear. If something in your chart is difficult, you'll hear it plainly — but you won't be told you're in danger and need to pay to fix it.
  • Push remedies. I don't sell gemstones, which means I have no reason to recommend one.
  • Take bookings that won't help. If a lawyer, therapist or financial adviser is what you need, I'll say so.
  • Influence another person's will. Relationship work is about your side of it, always.

How I work

Every session is one-on-one, private, and in Hindi or English — whichever you think in. It's online, by phone or video, which means the same access whether you're in a metro or a small town.

I keep the number of sessions per day limited. A reading given when I'm rushed is worse than no reading, and I'd rather turn down a booking than deliver one badly.

My job isn't to tell you what will happen. It's to make the situation clear enough that you can decide.
