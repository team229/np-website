12 Questions That Make a Tarot Reading Actually Useful

The quality of a reading is set before the cards are drawn. Here's how to ask something that will actually produce clarity.

Updated 2026-10-24By Nisha Pathak8 min read

In seven years of readings, the single biggest predictor of whether a session is useful isn't the spread, the deck, or the day. It's the question.

"Will he come back?" produces a shallow reading, because it's a yes/no question about someone else's behaviour. "What am I not seeing about this relationship?" produces a deep one, because it's about something you can actually act on.

The reframe

A good question has three qualities: it's about something you can influence, it's specific enough to explore, and it's honest about what you're actually feeling.

Most people arrive with a question that fails all three. That's not a criticism — it's just how anxiety works. It narrows attention onto the one thing you can't control.

For love and relationships

  1. "What am I not seeing about this situation?" — the workhorse question. Almost always produces something.
  2. "What is this person actually able to offer right now, as things stand?" — different from "what do they feel", and far more useful.
  3. "What am I avoiding by staying in this?" — uncomfortable, and usually the real question.
  4. "What would it look like if I chose myself here?" — useful when you already know.
  5. "How long am I willing to wait, and what would change my mind?" — turns an indefinite wait into a decision with edges.

For career and decisions

  1. "Is the problem the role, the manager, or the industry?" — these need different exits.
  2. "What am I getting from staying that I haven't admitted?" — usually safety, or familiarity, or avoiding a conversation.
  3. "What would I need to see to feel safe leaving?" — turns fear into an actual checklist.
  4. "What's the cost of another six months of this?" — the question people avoid, and the one that usually moves them.

For personal direction

  1. "What pattern keeps repeating, and what is it asking me to learn?"
  2. "What am I pretending not to know?" — blunt, and effective.
  3. "What would I do if I believed I deserved better?" — often the whole reading in one question.

Questions to avoid

Not because they're forbidden, but because they produce little:

  • "Will he/she text me?" — reduces everything to one notification.
  • "When exactly will X happen?" — invites false precision.
  • "Does he love me?" — a yes/no about someone's interior state; unanswerable and unhelpful.
  • "Is this person the one?" — outsources a decision you have to make.

A reading can't give you control over another person's behaviour. What it can do is show you where you actually have agency — which is usually more than you think, and rarely where you were looking.

If you're not sure what to ask

That's completely fine, and it's common. Tell me what's been on your mind and we'll shape it into a question together at the start of the session. The shaping is often half the value.
